Yoga for the Development of Concentration 
1 Introduction: 
 In our modern, fast-paced lives, our minds often wander, and we may find it challenging to 
stay focused and present. Yoga offers tools and practices  to cultivate mindfulness and sharpen our 
mental faculties. Attention and concentration are essential components of yoga. By focusing our 
attention on the breath, the body, or a specific object of meditation, we can train the mind to 
become more focused and concentrated. This enhanced mental clarity and concentration can have 
a positive impact on various aspects of our lives, including studies, work, relationships, and 
personal growth. 
2 Importance of Attention and Awareness 
2.1 Attention: Attention refers to the cognitive process of selectively focusing on a particular 
stimulus or object while filtering out other stimuli. It involves directing your awareness to a 
specific point of focus or sensory input, such as a sound, visual cue, thought , or physical 
sensation. Attention can be both voluntary and involuntary, and it determines what information 
enters our awareness at any given moment.  
 A man of ordinary intellect with highly developed attention can turn out more work than a 
highly intell ectual man who has a poor attention. Failure in anything is mainly due to lack of 
attention. If you attend to one thing at a time, you will get profound knowledge of that subject in 
its various aspects. The ordinary untrained man of the world generally att ends to several things at 
a time. He allows many things to enter the gates of his mental factory. That is the reason why he 
has a clouded or turbid mind. Through attention a deeper impression of anything is made in the 
mind. If you have good attention, you  can attend to the matter in hand exclusively. An attentive 
man has very good memory, and is very vigilant and circumspect. Attention can be cultivated and 
developed by persistent practice. There is great attention if the object is very pleasing. If not, you 
will have to create interest. Only t hen there will be attention. If the attention gets diminished, 
change your attention to another pleasant object. By patient training , you can direct the mind to 
attend to an unpleasant object also by creating interest. Then your will can be strengthened. 
2.2 Awareness: Awareness in yoga refers to a state of conscious presence and attentiveness to the 
present moment. It involves observing and perceiving the experiences happening within and 

--- PAGE 2 ---
around oneself without judgment or attachment. This includes being aware of the physical 
sensations, breath, thoughts, emotions, and the environment. Cultivating awareness allows 
individuals to develop a deeper understanding of themselves, their patterns of behavior, and their 
reactions to various stimuli. It encourages a non -judgmental and compassionate approach to self -
observation and self-discovery. 
 While awareness and attention are distinct, they complement each other in yoga practice. 
Developing awareness allows individuals  to observe their thoughts, emotions, and physical 
sensations with a non -judgmental attitude. It helps in understanding the interconnectedness of the 
mind and body. Attention, on the other hand, helps to refine and strengthen the focus of the mind, 
heighten concentration, and direct awareness to specific aspects of the practice. 
2.3 Current Challenges and Sensory Overload 
 Sensory overload is when your five senses —eyes, ears, tongue, skin, and nose —takes 
more information tha n what  your brain can process . Overwhelmed by all the input, the brain 
responds as it would to a life-threatening situation and enters into a fight, flight, or freeze mode. 
 You manage to do many activities with full attention, but sometimes there is no mental 
relaxation. And there ar e other moments , when your awareness seems to have expanded, but the 
mind is not sharp. In such condition, yoga can help to achieve the balanced state of awareness that 
can give you the clarity and sharpness of mind, as well as calmness and joy.  Faced with certain 
challenges, he or she knows how to h andle failure with a cceptance (Samatvam yoga uchyate  
Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2, verse 48). 
2.4 Take charge of your mind with awareness 
But why is awareness important? When you act without awareness – you are like a sleepwalker, 
who doesn’t know where he is walking. You just act on the strongest impulse – your mind 
controls you. However, when you act with awareness, you are in control of your mind. Your 
senses become so clear that they can perceive better, see better, think better, hear better. 
2.5 Methods to Improve Self- Awareness Through Yoga 
Body scan: Begin by bringing your attention to different parts of your body, starting from the toes 
and moving up to the head. Notice any sensations, tensions, or areas of comfort. This practice 
enhances your awareness of physical sensations and helps you connect with your body. 
Mindful movement with asanas: Engage in asanas mindfully by paying attention to the physical 
sensations, alignment, and breath. Stay present with each pose, observing how your body feels,

and being aware of any thoughts or emotions that arise. Mindful movement brings your attention 
to the present moment and strengthens the mind-body connection. 
Breath awareness through pranayama: Focus your attention on the breath, observing its natural 
rhythm, depth, and quality. Notice the sensation of the breath as it enters and leaves your body. 
This practice helps cultivate present-moment awareness and calms the mind, allowing you to 
connect with your inner state. 
Meditation: Regular meditation practice deepens self-awareness. Choose a meditation technique 
such as mindfulness meditation or focused-attention meditation. During meditation, observe your 
thoughts, emotions, and bodily sensations without judgment or attachment. This practice helps 
you become more aware of your mental and emotional patterns. 
Self-reflection: Dedicate time for self-reflection. Ask yourself introspective questions, such as 
"How am I feeling today?" or "What patterns or habits am I noticing?" Self-reflection allows you 
to explore your inner landscape and develop a deeper understanding of your thoughts, emotions, 
and behaviors.Yoga streamlines the thoughts and creates effectiveness in words and deeds. 
Yoga philosophy study: Dive into the study of yoga philosophy and wisdom texts like the Yoga 
Sutras of Patanjali or the Bhagavad Gita. Reflect on the teachings and contemplate how they 
apply to your life. This study deepens your understanding of yoga's principles and fosters self-
inquiry. 
3 Concentration 
“Concentration” is a word derived from two Latin words, i.e. “con,” a prefix meaning “to” and 
“centrum,” meaning “center” or “fixed central point.” Concentration thus refers to the sustained 
and intense mental effort directed towards a specific task, object, or goal. It involves maintaining 
focused attention over an extended period and resisting distractions. Concentration is often 
associated with a single -pointed focus, where the mind remains fixed on a particular subject or 
activity while excluding unrelated thoughts or stimuli. It is a deeper level of attention that requires 
mental discipline and engagement. 
3.1 Methods to Improve Concentration 
 The mind is a very restless thing, and its tendency is to dance from one thing to another, 
darting here and there, like a spoiled child, seeking a new object upon which to exercise itself. On 
the other hand, many people al low their minds to concentrate involuntarily upon whatever may 
strike their fancy, and, forgetting everything else, often neg lect duties and important interests, 

--- PAGE 4 ---
becoming daydreamers instead of firm thinkers. This involuntary concentration is a thing to be 
avoided. 
 The practice of concentration  consists of focusing the mind upon a given subject, or 
object, firmly and fixedly, and then holding it there for a certain time, fully intent upon its object, 
and not allowing itself to be diverted or attracted from its object.  
3.2 General Techniques to Develop Concentration 
 Concentration is very important in day to day life . Scientists discovered that the ability to 
stay focused can be an important indicator of a person’s future success. According to an old Zen 
proverb, “When walking, walk. When eating, eat”. This quote is not only about walking or eating. 
It describes an approach to life. It simply states that the most important thing is to focus your 
attention on present activity.  
 In general, adopting a healthy life style and exercises  to improve focus, awareness, and 
mental discipline may help sharpen your concentration .You can also boost your concentration by 
doing some simple exercises. 
Sleep well: Insufficient amounts of sleep impede your concentration. Constant sleep deprivation 
will leave you unable to focus and negative ly affect your mood and overall performance. To 
improve your sleep, it is advisable to maintain a regular schedule  of going to bed  early at night  
and getting up early in the morning. One should make a habit of sleeping at least 7  to 9 hours at 
night to maintain a sound health. 
Eat healthy food to avoid restless mind : A balanced diet is very important for your ability to stay 
focused. A healthy diet provides your brain with vitamins and minerals that allow it to function 
properly which can help you boost your focus, concentration and productivity. Even the quality of 
food has direct impact on the mind. One must consume food which should have soothing effect on 
the body and mind. 
Spending time in nature : Your brain has a tendency to relax in a natural environment  and this 
helps to increase concentration. Nature is the source of all beauties and boundless joy. That is why 
it is good to try and find time to spend with nature. You can go for a brisk walk in a park, bush, or 
alongside the sea -shore every day. Try to reserve 20 to 30 minutes in your daily schedule to be  
with nature. 
Love the subject or make innovative ways to make the subject interesting : Developing a love for a 
subject can be a transformative experience that leads to a deeper understanding and appreciation.

Turn the subject into a game or competition. Create rewards or incentives to keep you motivated 
and excited about the subject matter.  Connect the subject to real -life scenarios and practical

Understanding the science of pain and pleasure: According to yoga philosophy, pain (dukha) and 
pleasure (sukha) are both transient and impermanent experiences that arise from the interplay of 
various factors, including sensory stimuli, thoughts, emotions, and past conditioning. Yoga 
emphasizes that attachment to pleasure and aversion to pain create suffering and hinder the path to 
self-realization. It is the mind that labels experiences as either ple asurable or painful based on 
conditioning, expectations, and beliefs.  Yoga highlights the importance of cultivating a balanced 
and equanimous mind that can observe pain and pleasure without getting entangled or identified 
with them. Yoga teaches that true and lasting bliss (ananda) lies within, beyond the realm of pain 
and pleasure. Through various practices, such as asanas, pranayama, meditation, and self -inquiry, 
individuals can access a deeper sense of inner peace and contentment that transcends external  
circumstances. 
3.3 Yogic Ways to Improve Concentration 
Yoga offers several practices to improve concentration and focus. Here are some yogic techniques 
that can enhance your ability to concentrate: 
Asana practice: Regular practice of yoga asanas (postures) can help improve concentration. 
Asanas promote physical strength, flexibility, and balance, which in turn have a positive impact 
on mental clarity and focus. Specific asanas like Balasana (Child's Pose), Padmasana (Lotus 
Pose), and Sirsasana (Headstand) are known for their concentration-enhancing benefits. 
Pranayama: Pranayama, or yogic breathing exercises, can significantly improve concentration by 
calming the mind and balancing the energy in the body. Practices like Nadi Shodhana (Alternate 
Nostril Breathing) and Kapalabhati (Skull Shining Breath) help to purify the energy channels and 
increase mental clarity. 
Pratyahara (withdrawal of senses): Pratyahara is the practice of withdrawing the senses from 
external distractions and turning inward. By reducing the influence of external stimuli, you can 
redirect your attention and concentrate on your chosen object or task more effectively. Practices 
like Yoga Nidra (yogic sleep) and guided visualizations can help develop Pratyahara and improve 
concentration. 

--- PAGE 6 ---
Dharana (concentration) techniques: Dharana is a practice of focused concentration. By choosing 
an object or point of focus and directing your attention to it, you can train your mind to stay 
attentive and single-pointed. One simple technique is Trataka, which involves gazing steadily at a 
candle flame, an object, or a specific point. Regular practice of dharana helps to improve 
concentration and develop a calm, focused mind. 
Meditation: Meditation is a powerful practice to enhance concentration and cultivate mindfulness. 
Mindfulness meditation involves observing the breath, bodily sensations, thoughts, or emotions 
with non-judgmental awareness. By consistently practicing meditation, you can develop the 
ability to stay present and focused in everyday activities, improving overall concentration. 
Mantra chanting: Chanting mantras can be a powerful tool to enhance concentration. By repeating 
a specific sound, word, or phrase, you can focus the mind and cultivate a sense of inner stillness. 
The repetition of mantras, or other sacred sounds, helps to quiet the mind and improve 
concentration. 
Mindfulness: Bring mindfulness and full presence to your daily activities. Whether it's eating, 
walking, or working, engage in the present moment with full awareness. By consciously directing 
your attention to the task at hand, you can improve concentration and prevent the mind from 
wandering.

 Mind Taming Techniques 
 The practice of Yoga is kno wn to yoke the wandering mind. It relieves strain on the mi nd.

Hold on to commitments: Holding on to commitments is an important aspect of personal growth 
and achieving your goals. Clearly define your commitments and make them specific and 
measurable. Develop a detailed plan of action that outlines the steps you need to take to fulfill 
your commitments. Break them down into smaller, manageable tasks, and establish deadlines for 
each one. Discipline is crucial when holding on to commitments. Cultivate self-discipline by

establishing a routine, sticking to your plan, and resisting the temptation to deviate from your

Fulfill your promises: Fulfilling promises is essential for building trust, maintaining healthy 
relationships, and upholding personal integrity. Before making a promise, take a moment to 
consider if you can realistically fulfill it. Prioritize your commitments and allocate time and 
resources accordingly. If circumstances change and you're unable to fulfill a promise as initially 
agreed, communicate openly and promptly with the person involved. Develop a reputation for 
reliability by honoring your commitments and being punctual. Respect other people's time and 
expectations by fulfilling your promises on or before the agreed-upon deadline. 
Delayed gratification: Delayed gratification refers to the ability to resist immediate temptations or 
rewards in order to obtain a greater, more significant reward in the future. It involves exercising 
self-control and patience, often sacrificing short-term pleasure or desires for long-term benefits or 
goals. By practicing delayed gratification, you develop better decision-making skills, mental 
resilience and emotional strength. 
Intuition – ability to use the 6th sense: Developing intuition is a process of honing your inner 
knowing and accessing deeper insights and understanding beyond logical reasoning. It involves 
tapping into your innate wisdom and connecting with your inner guidance. Intuition often 
manifests as gut feelings or subtle nudges. Learn to trust these instincts and act upon them. Create 
moments of stillness and silence to quiet the mind. Engage in activities that ignite your passion 
and curiosity. When you follow your heart's desires, you align with your true self and open the 
channels for intuitive insights to flow. 
Willpower: Self-discipline is a key aspect of willpower. Start by identifying areas where you 
struggle with self-control and make a conscious effort to practice discipline in those areas. For 
example, if you struggle with procrastination, commit to completing tasks promptly. 
Set clear and specific goals: Clearly define what you want to achieve and make sure your goals 
are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART goals). This clarity will 
give you a clear direction and a sense of purpose, making it easier to exercise willpower. Create 
daily habits and rituals that align with your goals, and make a consistent effort to follow them. 
Self-respect and a positive self-image: When you live in accordance with your values, you can 
maintain a sense of integrity and self-respect. Treat yourself with kindness and understanding. 
Choose to spend time with people who support and uplift you, rather than those who bring you 
down or encourage negative behaviors.

Neck exercises 
     
Sit in a cross-legged pose with the hands resting on the knees in jnana or chin mudra. Close the 
eyes. Stage 1: Exhale, slowly move the head forward and try to touch the chin to the chest. Inhale, 
move the head as far back as is comfortable. This is one round. Practise 5-10 rounds. 
Stage 2: Exhale, slowly move the head to the right, bringing the right ear close to the right 
shoulder without raising the shoulders. Inhale, bring the head to the centre. Exhale, move the head 
to the left side and bring the left ear close to the left shoulder. This is one round. Practise 5-10 
rounds. 
Stage 3: Exhale, slowly turn the head to the right so that the chin is in line with the right shoulder. 
Inhale, bring the head to the centre. Exhale, move the head to the left side and bring the chin in 
line with the left shoulder. This is one round. Practise 5-10 rounds. 
Stage 4: Slowly rotate the neck in clockwise direction for 5-10 rounds and anticlockwise direction 
for 5-10 rounds.

Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutation) 
Step 1: Pranamasana (Prayer pose) 
 
Stand at the edge of the mat, keep the feet together. Breathing in, lift both arms up from the sides, 
and breathing out, bring the palms together in front of the chest in a prayer position. 
Step 2: Hastauttanasana (Raised arms pose) 
 
Breathing in, lift the arms up over the head and back, keeping the biceps close to the ears. In this 
pose, the effort is to stretch the whole body up from the heels to the tips of the fingers. 
Step 3: Padahastasana (Standing forward bend) 
 
Breathing out, bend forward from the waist keeping the spine erect. On exhalation completely, 
bring the hands down to the floor beside the feet.

Step 7: Bhujangasana (Cobra pose) 
 
Slide forward and raise the chest up into the Cobra pose. Keep the elbows bent in this pose with 
the shoulders away from the ears. Look up at the ceiling.

Virabhadrasana-II (Warrior Pose) 
 
Procedure: Stand in samasthiti. Angle the left foot 45 degrees. Take a wide step forward with the 
right foot. Bend the right knee. Inhale, take both arms up and extend the arms toward the front and 
back of the mat and turn the head to look past the right fingertips. Reverse the movements to come 
back to the starting pose. Repeat the above steps with the left foot forward. Practise 2-3 rounds 
each leg. 
Benefits: It strengthens and tones the arms, legs and lower back. It improves the balance of the 
body and helps increase stamina. It is beneficial for those with sedentary or deskbound jobs. It is 
beneficial in the case of frozen shoulders. It releases stress in the shoulders very effectively in a 
short period. 
Contraindications: Persons with knee issues should avoid this asana.

Gomukhasana (cow’s face pose) 
 
Procedure: Sit in dandasana. Bend the right leg and place the right feet under the left buttock. 
Stack the left knee over the right knee. Place both hands on the left knee. Close the eyes and relax.  
Benefits: Gomukhasana is an excellent asana for inducing relaxation. If practised for 10 minutes 
or more, it will alleviate tiredness, tension and anxiety. It alleviates cramp in the legs and makes 
the leg muscles supple. 
Contraindications: Those with severe pain in thighs, hip, knee, shoulder, hands and legs; injury 
to muscle, ligaments and tendons of legs; sprain in the legs; piles and knee injury should avoid 
this practice. 
Paschimottanasana (back stretching pose) 
 
Procedure: Sit on the floor with the legs outstretched, feet together and hands on the knees. This 
is the starting position. On inhalation raise the arms up and on exhalation bend forward from the 
hips. Try to grasp the big toes with the fingers and thumbs. Try to touch the knees with the 
forehead. This is the final position. Hold the position for as long as is comfortable and relax. 
Slowly return to the starting position. This is one round. Repeat two more rounds.

Setu Bandhasana (bridge pose) 
 
Procedure:  Lie on the back. Fold the knees and keep the feet hip distance apart on the floor, with 
knees and ankles in a straight line. Keep the arms beside the body, palms facing down. Inhaling, 
slowly lift the lower back, middle back and upper back off the floor; touch the chest to the chin 
without bringing the chin down, supporting the weight with the shoulders, arms and feet. Hold the 
posture for a comfortable duration and release this pose. 
Benefits: This pose strengthens the back muscles, relieves the tired back instantaneously, and 
gives a good stretch to the chest, neck and spine. It calms the brain, reducing anxiety, stress and 
depression. It opens up the lungs and reduces thyroid problems. It helps improve digestion and 
relieve the symptoms of menopause and menstrual pain. It is helpful in asthma, high blood 
pressure, osteoporosis, and sinusitis. 
Contraindications: Avoid doing this pose if one is suffering from neck and back injuries.

Veerasana (hero's pose) 
 
Procedure: Sit in dandasana. Fold the right knee and place the right foot beside the right hip with 
the tops of the feet flat on the floor. Place the left foot beside the left hip. Angle the big toes 
slightly in toward each other. Close the eyes and relax. Keep the body completely motionless and 
the spine and head straight. Hold for some time, then release the pose and relax the knees. 
Benefits: This asana balances the mind, increases the power of concentration, allows more 
awareness of the unconscious realms and induces physical and mental relaxation quickly. The 
thinking process becomes very clear and precise. It is useful for those who think too much or who 
have disturbed or uncontrollable thoughts. It is very good for the kidneys, liver, reproductive and 
abdominal organs. 
Contraindications: It should not be performed by people with weakness in the knees, stiff ankles, 
or inflammatory conditions such as arthritis, osteoarthritis, etc.

Chinmaya mudra (Gesture of Awareness) 
 
Procedure: Sit in any comfortable seating posture and place the hands with palm pointing 
upwards on the thighs or the knees. Meditation postures like padmasana or vajrasana are ideal for 
the practice of mudras. Close the eyes and take some deep breaths with the awareness of the 
breathing process. Now fold the index finger, press it with the thumb. The rest of the three fingers 
(middle, ring and small) should be curled into the palms. Place the palms on the upper thighs and 
palm facing forward. In addition, it should be done with both hands simultaneously. 
Benefits: This mudra promotes awareness of body and mind, and relieves stress and anxiety. It 
promotes mid-section of the lungs breathing. It helps insomnia and improves memory. This mudra 
also helps in dealing with stomach-related complaints. It can be practiced for 15-45 minutes.

Chandra Bheda Pranayama (left nostril breathing) 
 
Procedure: Assume a comfortable meditation asana. Place the hands on the knees in either chin 
or jnana mudra. Close the eyes and relax the whole body. Adopt nasagra mudra with the right 
hand. Close the right nostril with the thumb finger and inhale slowly and deeply through the left 
nostril. Exhale slowly through the right nostril, keeping the left nostril closed. This is one round. 
Practise 10 rounds. 
Benefits: Helpful in reducing body heat, heart burning problems, and high blood pressure. It 
reduces the tension, stress and other mental problems. 
Contraindications: Do not perform this pranayama in case of asthama, low blood pressure, 
cough and cold, and problems related to the respiratory system.  
Quick Relaxation Technique 
1. Rest in Shavasana. Observe the breath. Observe the breath in abdomen. Inhale, let the 
navel come up; Exhale, navel down. Slow and deep inhalation and exhalation.

Neck exercises 
     
Sit in a cross-legged pose with the hands resting on the knees in jnana or chin mudra. Close the 
eyes. Stage 1: Exhale, slowly move the head forward and try to touch the chin to the chest. Inhale, 
move the head as far back as is comfortable. This is one round. Practise 5-10 rounds. 
Stage 2: Exhale, slowly move the head to the right, bringing the right ear close to the right 
shoulder without raising the shoulders. Inhale, bring the head to the centre. Exhale, move the head 
to the left side and bring the left ear close to the left shoulder. This is one round. Practise 5-10 
rounds. 
Stage 3: Exhale, slowly turn the head to the right so that the chin is in line with the right shoulder. 
Inhale, bring the head to the centre. Exhale, move the head to the left side and bring the chin in 
line with the left shoulder. This is one round. Practise 5-10 rounds. 
Stage 4: Slowly rotate the neck in clockwise direction for 5-10 rounds and anticlockwise direction 
for 5-10 rounds.

Baddha Konasana (bound angle pose) 
 
Procedure: Sit with legs straight out in front. Exhale and bend the knees, and pull the heels 
towards the pelvis. Drop the knees out to the sides and press the soles of feet together. Bring the 
heels as close to pelvis as possible. Grab the big toe to each foot with first two fingers and thumb. 
Stay in the pose for 1-5 minutes.  
Benefits: This asana stimulates abdominal organs, ovaries, bladder and kidneys. It Improves 
blood circulation, stretches the inner thighs, and helps to relieve mild depression, anxiety and 
fatigue. It particularly relieves the symptoms of menopause. 
Contraindications: Those with knee injury can avoid this practice.

Ushtrasana (camel pose) 
 
Procedure: Sit in vajrasana. Stand on the knees with the arms at the sides. The knees and feet 
should be together, but may be separated if this is more comfortable. Lean backward, slowly 
reaching for the right heel with the right hand and then the left heel with the left hand. Push the 
hips forward, keeping the thighs vertical, and bend the head and spine backward as far as is 
comfortable. Remain in the final position for as long as is comfortable. Return to the starting 
position by slowly releasing the hands from the heels one at a time. 
Benefits: This asana is beneficial for the digestive and reproductive systems. It stretches the 
stomach and intestines, alleviating constipation. The backward bend loosens up the vertebrae and 
stimulates the spinal nerves, relieving backache, rounded back and drooping shoulders. The 
posture is improved. The front of the neck is fully stretched, toning the organs in this region and 
regulating the thyroid gland. It is helpful for people suffering from asthma. 
Contraindications: People with severe back ailments such as lumbago should not attempt this 
asana. 
Jatara Parivrittasana (revolved abdominal twist pose) 
 


--- PAGE 27 ---
Procedure: Lie in shavasana. Bend the knees and place the soles of both feet flat on the floor, 
directly in front of the buttocks. Keep the knees and feet together throughout the practice. Place 
both hands in-line with the shoulders, with the palms touching the floor. Breathe in, and while 
breathing out, slowly lower the legs to the right, trying to bring the knees down to the floor. At the 
same time, gently turn the head and neck in the opposite direction to the legs. This will give a 
uniform twisting stretch to the entire spine. Hold the breath in the final position while mentally 
counting three seconds. While breathing in, raise both legs to the upright position. Keep the 
shoulders and elbows on the floor throughout. Repeat on the left side to complete one round. 
Practise 5 complete rounds. 
Benefits: This asana gives an excellent stretch to the abdominal muscles and organs, and thereby 
helps to improve digestion and eliminate constipation. The twisting stretch of the spinal muscles 
relieves the strain and stiffness caused by prolonged sitting. 
Contraindications: People recovering from abdominal surgery and injury to ankles, knees, neck, 
spine, hips, and shoulders should avoid this asana. 
Apanasana (knees to chest pose) 
 
Procedure: Lie in shavasana. On an exhale, hug the knees into the chest and clasp the hands 
around the front of them. To release, take an inhale, extend the legs out and bring the arms to rest 
alongside the body. 
Benefits: This asana relieves gas, stimulates digestion, stretches the lower back, and clams the 
mind. 
Contraindications: People having hernia, and those recovering from abdominal surgery and 
injury to spine, hips, and knee should avoid this asana.

Simhasana (lion pose) 
 
Procedure: Sit in vajrasana. The toes of both feet should remain in contact with each other. Place 
the hands on the knees. Straighten the arms fully. Open the mouth wide and stick out the 
tongue as far as possible. At the same time, roll the eyes up and focus at the center between 
the eyebrows.  Stay for a few seconds and relax the face. Repeat three times.  
Benefits: This pose provides a good exercise to the facial muscles and improves circulation of 
blood to the face. This posture, with the mouth wide open, aids in better functioning of the carotid 
sinus nerve. This pose also aids in the functioning of the thyroid and parathyroid glands and 
brings smoothing effect to the eyes and helps in reducing eye strain. It brings calmness to the 
mind by removing the stress. 
Contraindications: Those with knee and eye conditions should avoid this asana.

Surya Bheda Pranayama (vitality stimulating breath) 
 
Procedure: Assume a comfortable meditation asana. Place the hands on the knees in either chin

Benefits: This practice creates heat in the body. By increasing extroversion and dynamism, it
