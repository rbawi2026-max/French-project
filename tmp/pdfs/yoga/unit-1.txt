3.1 Brain and its Functions  
 Brain is part of the Central Nervous System (CNS). It lies in the central cavity and 
consists of cerebrum, cerebellum, mid brain, pons, and medulla oblongata. Although the brain is 
only about 2% of the total body weight in humans, it receives 15-20% of the  body's blood 
supply. The brain has to be well provided with oxygen; because lack of oxygen destroys the 
brain cells, which once destroyed cannot be regenerated. Constant physical and mental tension 
lead to destruction of the brain cells and reduction in t heir numbers. This affects concentration 
and memory adversely.  
The major parts of the Brain: 
1-Cerebrum (It includes thalamus and hypothalamus) 
2-Cerebellum (also known as the ‘little brain’) 
3- Brain Stem (It has 3 parts: midbrain, pons and Medulla Oblongata). 
3.1.1 The Cerebrum:  It is the major and largest part of the brain made up of two cerebral 
hemispheres (right and left) connected by a structure called corpus callosum. Each cerebral

hemisphere is made up of four lobes: frontal, temporal, parietal an d occipital lobes. The outer 
portion of the cerebrum or cortex is the site of the mind and intelligence. It is also called the grey 
matter. This is our conscious brain which allows us to think, feel, and decide our movements. It 
governs the whole body. Thi s part of the brain consists of thalamus and hypothalamus. The 
thalamus is an important sensory information receiving center. All the sensory inputs except 
smell enter into the thalamus and sent to the concerned area of the grey matter. The 
hypothalamus is located below the thalamus. It performs many of the important tasks, both for 
survival and for enjoyment of life. It functions as a link between the mind (psyche) and body 
(soma). It also links the nervous system with the endocrine system. 
The main functions of hypothalamus as under: 
It regulates the autonomic nervous system, the blood pressure, heart rate, digestion, respiration, 
body temperature, emotions, thirst, food intake, neuroendocrine link, sleep-wake cycle. 
3.1.2 The Cerebellum:  The cerebellum is  located under the back portion the cerebrum and is 
partially covered by it. It is the second largest part of the brain. It is connected to many parts of 
the brain and has important role in coordinating movements. It helps us in controlling our muscle 
movements, balance and equilibrium. 
3.1.3 The Brain Stem:  The mid brain, pons, and medulla oblongata together form brain stem 
which houses the cerebral hemispheres on its top and bears cerebellum on its back. Besides 
having it special own special features, the  brain stem acts like corridor for the nerve fibers going 
to and coming from different parts of the central nervous system connecting one region with 
another for coordination purpose. When the medulla is injured, death results, the heart and lungs 
stop beating and breathing respectively. 
The medulla oblongata is the portion of the brain stem. It connects nerve fibers of the brain and 
the spinal cord. All sensory and motor nerves pass through the medulla oblongata as they relay 
information between the brain and the spinal cord. The medulla oblongata, pons and cerebellum 
are collectively known as the hind brain. 
3.2 Autonomic Nervous System 
 Autonomic nervous system controls the functions of the glands, blood vessels and organs 
of the body. Autonomic nervous system has two divisions known as sympathetic and 
parasympathetic systems . These two subdivisions perform the opposite actions for all the

3.2.1 Sympathetic nervous system: 
 It is the part of the autono mic nervous system located near the thoracic and lumbar 
regions in the spinal cord. Its primary function is to prepare the body for an emergency situation, 
and stimulate the body’s fight -or-flight response. It does this by regulating the heart rate, rate o f 
respiration, pupillary response and more.  
3.2.2 Parasympathetic nervous system 
 The parasympathetic nerves generally extend from the brainstem and the lower regions of 
the spinal cord. It is responsible for promoting rest, relaxation, and restoration in  the body. The 
main function of the parasympathetic nervous system is to counterbalance the "fight -or-flight" 
response initiated by the sympathetic nervous system, bringing the body back to a state of 
equilibrium. When the parasympathetic nervous system is  activated, it slows down heart rate, 
decreases blood pressure, and promotes digestion and absorption of nutrients. It conserves 
energy and promotes bodily processes that facilitate rest and recovery. This state is often referred 
to as the "rest and digest " or "feed and breed" response. Activities that promote parasympathetic 
activation include deep breathing, meditation, gentle stretching, and engaging in activities that 
induce relaxation and calmness.

3. States of Mind (as Per Sankhya and Yogic Philosophy) 
 To reach the state of absolute calmness and balance, we must first understand the 
different states of mind. The five states of mind according to the Yoga Sutras: 
1. Kshipta (Distracted Mind or Monkey Mind): Kshipta is a state of mind characterized by 
constant distraction, restlessness, and a lack of focus. In this state, the mind is easily swayed 
by external stimuli, wandering from one thought to another. It is akin to a turbulent and 
agitated mind that finds it challenging to maintain sustained attention. Kshipta is considered 
an obstacle to deep concentration and inner awareness. 
2. Mudha (Dull Mind or Donkey Mind): Mudha refers to a dull, lethargic, or stupefied state of 
mind. It is associated with a lack of clarity, confusion, and a general state of mental dullness. 

--- PAGE 9 ---
In this state, the mind may feel sluggish and unmotivated, lacking the energy and clarity 
needed for focused attention or productive engagement. Because of this lack of drive, a 
person in this state of mind often give up and feel hopeless when situations are challenging, 
resulting in failure and dissatisfaction. 
3. Vikshipta (Semi-focused Mind or Butterfly Mind): Vikshipta is a state where the mind 
oscillates between periods of attention and distraction. It is characterized by a somewhat 
scattered or wavering focus. In this state, the mind may experience moments of concentration 
but is easily pulled away by distractions or external stimuli. It is a state where there is some 
effort made to concentrate, but the mind is not yet fully stable. 
4. Ekagra (One-pointed Mind): Ekagra refers to a state of deep concentration and one-pointed 
focus. In this state, the mind becomes steady, calm, and fully absorbed in the object of 
attention. It is a state of heightened focus, where the mind is undistracted and fully engaged 
in the present moment. Ekagra is considered conducive to meditation and inner exploration. 
5. Niruddha (Mastered Mind): Niruddha is the state of a fully mastered or controlled mind. It 
represents a state of complete stillness and tranquility, where the mind is no longer disturbed 
by external or internal distractions. Niruddha is associated with a state of profound inner 
peace and liberation, where the fluctuations of the mind have been transcended. When the 
Yogi sustains the state of niruddha for long periods, the mind reaches a state of complete 
liberation (samadhi). The niruddha state of mind is the ultimate goal of Yoga. 
The aim of yoga practice is to cultivate and move towards the state of Ekagra and ul timately 
Niruddha, where the mind becomes more focused, steady, and liberated from distractions.

Beta: This brainwave is associated with mental alertness, focus, and concentration. It is 
characterized by oscillations in the frequency range of approximately 12 to 30 Hz.  We are in the 
Beta state during tasks such as problem -solving, studying, working, analytical thinking, logical 
reasoning, critical analysis or participating in stimulating conversations. Excessive beta activity 
can be related to increased stress, anxiety, or overthinking. 
Alpha: Alpha waves are commonly observed when a person is in a state of wakeful relaxation, or 
a calm and focused mental state. It has a frequency range of approximately 8 to 13 Hz.  In this 
state, we are able to tap into the subconscious mind. Our subconscious mind is susceptible to 
receive messages from our conscious mind. Therefore, it is essential to learn to tap into the alpha 
state of mind so that we can reprogram our subconscious mind to our benefit. Practicing yogic 
relaxation such as yoga nidra, mindfulness practices, deep breathing exercises, and medit ation 
allow individuals to enter the alpha brain wave state more readily. 
Theta: The theta brain wave state is associated with dream sleep state. Key characteristics of the 
theta wave state is deep relaxation, enhanced creativity and imagination, memory co nsolidation, 
heightened intuition, and emotional regulation during light sleep. Theta waves have a frequency 
range of approximately 4 to 8 Hz. Theta can be intentionally accessed through meditation, 
hypnosis, guided visualization, or other relaxation techn iques to harness the mind’s potential for 
personal growth, creativity, and inner transformation. 
Delta: The delta brain wave state is a type of brainwave pattern associated with deep sleep and 
unconsciousness. It is the slowest and highest amplitude brain wave frequency, typically ranging 
from 0.5 to 4 Hz. During this stage, the body undergoes restorative processes, including tissue 
repair, hormone regulation, and memory consolidation. Though this state is primarily associated 
with deep sleep, it can also b e observed in states of deep meditation or transcendental 
experiences.

Procedure: Sit in a comfortable posture. Interlock the fingers with the thumbs straight. Bring the 
hands at the level of the eyes. Follow the tip of the thumbs with the eyes while following the

a. Upward and downward movement 
b. Side to side movement

Trikonasana (triangle pose) 
 
Variation I Procedure: Stand erect with the feet more than shoulder width apart. Turn the right 
foot to the right side. Stretch the arms sideways and raise them to shoulder level so that they are

hand on the right foot, keeping the two arms in line with each other. Turn the left palm forward.

straight line. Repeat on the other side. This completes one round. Practise 3 to 5 rounds.

reduce waistline fat.  
Contraindications: Avoid doing this pose if one is suffering from migraine, diarrhea, low or 
high blood pressure, or neck and back injuries.

Vrikshasana (tree pose) 
 
Procedure: Stand upright with the feet together and the arms at the sides. Focus the gaze on a 
fixed point in front of the body. Bend the right leg, grasp the ankle and place the sole of the foot 
on the inside of the left thigh. The heel should be close to the perineum and the right knee should 
point out to the side. When the body is balanced, raise the arms up over the head and hold the 
hands in prayer position. Release the hands and then the foot. Relax completely in the starting 
position, and change sides. 
Benefits: This pose induces a state of rejuvenation. It stretches the legs, back and arms, and 
invigorates the practitioner. It brings balance and equilibrium to the mind, and helps build focus 
and concentration. It helps improve concentration. It makes the legs strong, improves balance, 
and opens the hips. 
Contraindications: If one suffers from vertigo or migraine issues, then avoid doing this asana.

Kapalbhati Pranayama (frontal brain cleansing breath) 
 
Procedure: Sit in a comfortable meditation asana. The head and spine should be straight with 
the hands resting on the knees in either chin or jnana mudra. Close the eyes. Exhale through both 
nostrils with a forceful contraction of the abdominal muscles. The following inhalation should

Instant Relaxation Technique 
Procedure:

2. Join the legs and arms by the side of the body.

5. Release and relax in Shavasana.

7. Come back to the sitting position. 
Benefits: Slowing heart and breathing rate, lowering blood pressure and fatigue, reducing 
activity of stress hormones as well as reducing anger and frustration, increasing blood flow to 
major muscles and reducing muscle tension and chronic pain, and improving concentration, 
mood and sleep quality.

Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutation) 
Step 1: Pranamasana (Prayer pose) 
 
Stand at the edge of the mat, keep the feet together. On breathing in, lift both arms up from the 
sides, and on exhale, bring the palms together in front of the chest in a prayer position. 
Step 2: Hastauttanasana (Raised arms pose) 
 
Breathing in, lift the arms up over the head and back, keeping the biceps close to the ears. In this 
pose, the effort is to stretch the whole body up from the heels to the tips of the fingers. 
Step 3: Padahastasana (Standing forward bend) 
 


--- PAGE 25 ---
Breathing out, bend forward from the waist keeping the spine erect. On exhaling completely, 
bring the hands down to the floor beside the feet. 
Step 4: Ashwa Sanchalanasana (Equestrian pose) 
 
Breathing in, push the right leg back, as far back as possible. Bring the right knee to the floor and 
look up. 
Step 5: Dandasana (Stick pose) 
 
Breathing in, take the left leg back and bring the whole body in a straight line. 
Step 6: Ashtanga Namaskara (Salute with eight parts or points) 
 
Gently bring the knees down to the floor and exhale. Take the hips back slightly, slide forward, 
rest the chest and chin on the floor. Raise the posterior a little bit. The two hands, two feet, two 
knees, chest and chin (eight parts of the body) should touch the floor. 
Step 7: Bhujangasana (Cobra pose) 
 


--- PAGE 26 ---
Slide forward and raise the chest up into the Cobra pose. Keep the elbows bent in this pose with 
the shoulders away from the ears. Look up at the ceiling. 
Step 8: Parvatasana (Mountain pose) 
 
Breathing out, lift the hips and the tailbone up to bring the body into an inverted ‘V’ pose. 
Step 9: Ashwa Sanchalanasana (Equestrian pose) 
 
Breathing in, bring the right foot forward in between the two hands. The left knee goes down on 
the floor. Press the hips down and look up. 
Step 10: Padahastasana (Standing forward bend) 
 
Breathing out, bring the left foot forward. Keep the palms on the floor.  
Step 11: Hastauttanasana (Raised arms pose)

hips slightly outward. 
Step 12: Tadasana (Mountain pose) 
On exhalation, first straighten the body, then bring the arms down. Relax in this position and 
observe the sensations of the body.

Trikonasana (triangle pose) 
 
Variation II Procedure: Stand erect with the feet more than shoulder width apart and raise the 
arms sideways to shoulder level. This is the starting position. Bend forward. Twist the trunk to 
the right, bringing the left hand to the right foot. The right arm should be stretched vertically so 
that both arms form a straight line. Look up at the right hand. Hold the final position for a few 
seconds, feeling the twist and stretch of the back. Return to the centre forward position. Raise the 
body to the starting position, keeping the arms outstretched to the sides. Repeat on the other side. 
This is one round. Practise 3 to 5 rounds. 


--- PAGE 28 ---
Benefits: This asana affects the muscles on the sides of the trunk, the waist and the back of the 
legs. It stimulates the nervous system and alleviates nervous depression. It improves digestion. It 
also strengthens the pelvic area and tones the reproductive organs. Regular practice will help to 
reduce waistline fat.  
Contraindications: Avoid doing this pose if one is suffering from migraine, diarrhea, low or 
high blood pressure, or neck and back injuries.  
Utkatasana (chair pose) 
 
Procedure: Stand erect with the feet slightly apart. Stretch the hands to the front with palms 
facing downwards. Do not bend the elbows. Bend the knees and gently push the pelvis down as 
if one is sitting in an imaginary chair. Stay for a comfortable duration. Come back to starting 
position and relax. Practise 3 rounds. 
Benefits: This asana exercises the spine, hips and chest muscles. It helps strengthen the lower 
back and torso. It tones the thigh, ankle, leg and knee muscles. 
Contraindications: Do not practice this yoga posture if one has chronic knee pain, arthritis, 
sprained ankle; any knee problem or damaged ligaments; headache or insomnia (sleeplessness).

Garudasana (Eagle pose) 
 
Procedure: From samasthiti, bend the right leg and twist it around the left leg. Bend the elbows 
and bring them in front of the chest. Twist the forearms around each other with the left elbow 
remaining below. Place the palms together to resemble an eagle's beak. Slowly bend the left knee 
and lower the body, keeping the back straight, until the elbows come down to the knees and the 
tip of the right big toe touches the floor. Keep the eyes focused on the fixed point. Hold the final 
position for as long as is comfortable, then raise the body, and release the legs and arms. Repeat 
with the legs and arms the opposite way around. Practise up to 3 rounds on each side.  
Benefits: Garudasana improves concentration, strengthens the muscles and loosens the joints of 
the shoulders, arms and legs, and is good for the upper back. 
Contraindications: Those with knee injury and arthritis should avoid this asana. 
Naukasana (boat pose) 
 
Procedure: Sit in Dandasana. Bend the knees and place the feet on the floor. Extend the arms

Benefits: This asana stimulates the muscular, digestive, circulatory, nervous and hormonal

Bhujangasana with Variations (Cobra Pose) 
   
Procedure: Lie down in the prone position and bring both feet together. Bend the elbows and 
place the hands underneath the shoulders. (a) Inhale, raise only the neck. Exhale, bring the neck 
down. (b) Inhale, raise only the neck and shoulders. Exhale, bring the neck and shoulders down. 
(c) Inhale, raise the neck, shoulders, and upper torso by straightening the elbows. Exhale, bring 
the neck and upper torso down. (d) Place the hands near the chest. Inhale, raise the neck, 
shoulders, and upper torso by straightening the elbows. Exhale, bring the neck and upper torso 
down.      
Benefits: This asana improves and deepens breathing. It can help to remove backache and keep 
the spine supple and healthy. It tones the ovaries and uterus, and helps in menstrual and some 
other gynaecological disorders. It stimulates the appetite, alleviates constipation and is beneficial 
for all abdominal organs, especially the liver and kidneys.

Eka Pada Uthitha Padasana (raised single leg pose) 
 
Procedure: Lie in the starting position with the palms flat on the floor. Inhale and raise the right 
leg as high as is comfortable, keeping it straight and the foot relaxed. The left leg should remain 
straight and in contact with the floor. Hold the posture for 3 to 5 seconds. Exhale and slowly 
lower the leg to the floor. This is one round. Practise 5-10 rounds with the right leg and then 5-10 
rounds with the left leg. 
Benefits: This asana strengthens the abdominal muscles and massages the organs. It strengthens 
the digestive system, lower back, pelvic and perineal muscles and helps correct prolapse. 
Contraindications: Not to be performed by persons suffering from high blood pressure or 
serious back conditions such as sciatica and slipped disc. 
Ardha Shalabhasana (half locust pose) 
 
Procedure: Lie flat on the stomach with the hands under the thighs, palms downwards or hands 
clenched. Keep both the legs straight throughout the practice. Place the chin on the floor, slightly

Chin Mudra 
 
Procedure: Assume a comfortable meditation posture. Fold the index fingers so that they touch 
the tip of the thumbs. Straighten the other three fingers of each hand so that they are relaxed and 
slightly apart. Place the hands on the knees with the palms facing up. Relax the hands and arms. 
Benefits: Chin mudra is a simple but important psycho-neural finger lock which makes 
meditation asanas more powerful. The palms and fingers of the hands have many nerve root 
endings which constantly emit energy. When the index finger touches the thumb, a circuit is 
produced which allows the energy that would normally dissipate into the environment to travel 
back through the body and up to the brain. It promotes concentration, calms the mind, boosts 
memory and improves sleep.  
Sheetkari Pranayama (hissing breath) 


--- PAGE 33 ---
 
Procedure: Sit in any comfortable meditation posture. Close the eyes and relax the whole body. 
Hold the teeth lightly together. Separate the lips, exposing the teeth. The tongue may be kept flat 
or folded against the soft palate in khechari mudra. Inhale slowly and deeply through the teeth. 
At the end of the inhalation, close the mouth. Exhale slowly through the nose in a controlled 
manner. This is one round. Practise 10 rounds. 
Benefits: This practice cools the body. It reduces mental and emotional excitation. It induces 
muscular relaxation, mental tranquility and may be used as a tranquillizer before sleep. It gives 
control over hunger and thirst. 
Contraindications: People suffering from low blood pressure or respiratory disorders such as 
asthma, bronchitis and excessive mucous, should not practise this pranayama.
