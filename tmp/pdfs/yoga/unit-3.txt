Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutation) 
Step 1: Pranamasana (Prayer pose) 
 
Stand at the edge of the mat, keep the feet together. Breathing in, lift both arms up from the 
sides, and breathing out, bring the palms together in front of the chest in a prayer position. 
Step 2: Hastauttanasana (Raised arms pose) 
 
Breathing in, lift the arms up over the head and back, keeping the biceps close to the ears. In this 
pose, the effort is to stretch the whole body up from the heels to the tips of the fingers. 
Step 3: Padahastasana (Standing forward bend)

Step 4: Ashwa Sanchalanasana (Equestrian pose)

Step 6: Ashtanga Namaskara (Salute with eight parts or points) 
 
Gently bring the knees down to the floor and exhale. Take the hips back slightly, slide forward, 
rest the chest and chin on the floor. Raise the posterior a little bit. The two hands, two feet, two 
knees, chest and chin (eight parts of the body) should touch the floor.

Virabhadrasana-II (Warrior Pose) 
 
Procedure: Stand in samasthiti. Angle the left foot 45 degrees. Take a wide step forward with 
the right foot. Bend the right knee. Inhale, take both arms up and extend the arms toward the 
front and back of the mat and turn the head to look past the right fingertips. Reverse the 
movements to come back to the starting pose. Repeat the above steps with the left foot forward. 
Practise 2-3 rounds each leg. 
Benefits: It strengthens and tones the arms, legs and lower back. It improves the balance of the 
body and helps increase stamina. It is beneficial for those with sedentary or deskbound jobs. It is 
beneficial in the case of frozen shoulders. It releases stress in the shoulders very effectively in a 
short period. 
Contraindications: Persons with knee issues should avoid this asana.

Procedure: Sit in dandasana. Bend the right leg and place the right feet under the left buttock. 
Stack the left knee over the right knee. Place both hands on the left knee. Close the eyes and 
relax.  
Benefits: Gomukhasana is an excellent asana for inducing relaxation. If practised for 10 minutes 
or more, it will alleviate tiredness, tension and anxiety. It alleviates cramp in the legs and makes 
the leg muscles supple. 
Contraindications: Those with severe pain in thighs, hip, knee, shoulder, hands and legs; injury 
to muscle, ligaments and tendons of legs; sprain in the legs; piles and knee injury should avoid 
this practice. 
Paschimottanasana (back stretching pose) 
 
Procedure: Sit on the floor with the legs outstretched, feet together and hands on the knees. This 
is the starting position. On inhalation raise the arms up and on exhalation bend forward from the 
hips. Try to grasp the big toes with the fingers and thumbs. Try to touch the knees with the 
forehead. This is the final position. Hold the position for as long as is comfortable and relax. 
Slowly return to the starting position. This is one round. Repeat two more rounds. 
Benefits: This asana stretches the hamstring muscles and increases flexibility in the hip joints. It 
tones and massages the entire abdominal and pelvic region, including the liver, pancreas, spleen, 
uro-genital system, kidneys and adrenal glands. It helps to remove excess weight in this area and 
stimulates circulation to the nerves and muscles of the spine. 
Contraindications: People who suffer from slipped disc, sciatica or hernia should not practise 
paschimottanasana.

Setu Bandhasana (bridge pose) 
 
Procedure:  Lie on the back. Fold the knees and keep the feet hip distance apart on the floor, 
with knees and ankles in a straight line. Keep the arms beside the body, palms facing down. 
Inhaling, slowly lift the lower back, middle back and upper back off the floor; touch the chest to 
the chin without bringing the chin down, supporting the weight with the shoulders, arms and feet. 
Hold the posture for a comfortable duration and release this pose. 
Benefits: This pose strengthens the back muscles, relieves the tired back instantaneously, and 
gives a good stretch to the chest, neck and spine. It calms the brain, reducing anxiety, stress and 
depression. It opens up the lungs and reduces thyroid problems. It helps improve digestion and 
relieve the symptoms of menopause and menstrual pain. It is helpful in asthma, high blood 
pressure, osteoporosis, and sinusitis. 
Contraindications: Avoid doing this pose if one is suffering from neck and back injuries. 
Veerasana (hero's pose) 
 
Procedure: Sit in dandasana. Fold the right knee and place the right foot beside the right hip 
with the tops of the feet flat on the floor. Place the left foot beside the left hip. Angle the big toes 
slightly in toward each other. Close the eyes and relax. Keep the body completely motionless and 
the spine and head straight. Hold for some time, then release the pose and relax the knees.

Benefits: This asana balances the mind, increases the power of concentration, allows more

Yogamudrasana (psychic union pose) 
 
Procedure: From dandasana adopt padmasana and close the eyes. Hold one wrist behind the 
back with the other hand. Inhale deeply. While exhaling, bend forward, keeping the spine 
straight. Bring the forehead to the floor or as close as is comfortable. Stay in the final position for 
as long as is comfortable. Slowly return to the starting position. Repeat the pose with the legs 
crossed the other way around in padmasana. 
Benefits: This is an excellent asana for massaging the abdominal organs, removing constipation 
and indigestion. It stretches the back, freeing the spinal nerves which emerge from the spaces 
between the vertebrae, thus contributing to good general health. 
Contraindications: People with serious eye, back or heart conditions, or with high blood 
pressure, and those in the early post-operative or post-delivery period should not attempt this 
asana. 
Chinmaya mudra (Gesture of Awareness)

Procedure: Sit in any comfortable seating posture and place the hands with palm pointing 
upwards on the thighs or the knees. Meditation postures like padmasana or vajrasana are ideal for 
the practice of mudras. Close the eyes and take some deep breaths with the awareness of the 
breathing process. Now fold the index finger, press it with the thumb. The rest of the three 
fingers (middle, ring and small) should be curled into the palms. Place the palms on the upper 
thighs and palm facing forward. In addition, it should be done with both hands simultaneously. 
Benefits: This mudra promotes awareness of body and mind, and relieves stress and anxiety. It 
promotes mid-section of the lungs breathing. It helps insomnia and improves memory. This 
mudra also helps in dealing with stomach-related complaints. It can be practiced for 15-45 
minutes.  
Chandra Bheda Pranayama (left nostril breathing) 
 
Procedure: Assume a comfortable meditation asana. Place the hands on the knees in either chin 
or jnana mudra. Close the eyes and relax the whole body. Adopt nasagra mudra with the right 
hand. Close the right nostril with the thumb finger and inhale slowly and deeply through the left 
nostril. Exhale slowly through the right nostril, keeping the left nostril closed. This is one round. 
Practise 10 rounds. 
Benefits: Helpful in reducing body heat, heart burning problems, and high blood pressure. It 
reduces the tension, stress and other mental problems. 
Contraindications: Do not perform this pranayama in case of asthama, low blood pressure, 
cough and cold, and problems related to the respiratory system.

Breath Awareness Meditation 
1. Sit in a comfortable meditative posture and adopt Chin mudra. 
2. Do a quick body awareness/scan to relax the body. 
3. Begin observing the natural in-breath and out-breath. 
4. Feel the cool in-breath and warm out-breath at the nostril. 
5. If any other thought arises, bring the mind back to the breath. 
6. Continue the practice for 2-5 minutes.
